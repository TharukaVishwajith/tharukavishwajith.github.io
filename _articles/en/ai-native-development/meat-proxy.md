> Put simply, a “meat proxy” is someone who passes other people's questions to AI and passes the answers back, without adding their own understanding, analysis or critical thinking.

**If AI can do the same job without us in the middle, what value are we adding?**

Today, AI can help us write code, do research, create reports and find information for decisions in seconds.

But there is a risk: as AI becomes more capable, our own ability to think independently may gradually weaken.

---

A **2025 study by researchers at Microsoft and Carnegie Mellon** found that greater confidence in AI was associated with less effort spent on critical thinking.

Through his concept of **Deep Work**, **Cal Newport** argues that the ability to concentrate and think deeply is becoming increasingly rare and valuable.

I think that idea matters even more in the AI era.

**Getting answers from AI and truly understanding those answers are two different things.**

The most valuable person in the future may not be the one who gets the most answers from AI.

It may be the person who can ask the right questions, think deeply, challenge AI's answers and use them to create new value.

Our education system, in particular, should teach more than how to use AI. It should also place greater emphasis on **independent thinking**, **critical thinking** and **deep focus**.

Let's use AI as a tool that strengthens our thinking.

---

There is a question we should keep asking ourselves:

> ### AI is getting smarter every day. Are we getting smarter as we use it?
